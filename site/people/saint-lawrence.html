Saint Lawrence

c. 225–258 · Early Church

Also known as Lawrence of Rome; Laurence; Laurentius; Lawrence the Deacon

Deacon of the church of Rome, martyred in the persecution under Valerian; patron of deacons, cooks, and the poor

One of the seven deacons of Rome under Pope Sixtus II, in charge of the church's goods and its care for the poor, who was executed on 10 August 258, four days after Sixtus, in the persecution ordered by the emperor Valerian. His date and tomb are well attested; the famous stories of his showing the poor as 'the treasures of the church' and joking on the gridiron come from Ambrose and Prudentius more than a century later and are labelled here as tradition. He became one of the most honoured martyrs of Rome.

What they did

  • Served as one of the seven deacons of Rome under Pope Sixtus II, responsible, according to Ambrose and Prudentius, for the church's goods and for distributing its alms to the poor, widows, and orphans.[3][4]
  • Was put to death on 10 August 258, four days after Sixtus, under Valerian's order that clergy be executed; his feast day and burial on the Via Tiburtina are recorded in the Roman list of martyrs' anniversaries preserved in the Chronography of 354, among the best-attested facts about him.[2][1]
  • By tradition, when the authorities demanded the church's treasure he asked for time, gave its money away, and brought the poor and sick, saying they were the treasures of the church. The story is first told by Ambrose (c. 388–391) and in Prudentius's hymn (c. 400), more than a century later.[3][4][9]
  • By tradition he was roasted on a gridiron and joked with his judge that one side was done and he should be turned over. Many historians doubt this, since Valerian's rescript called for the sword and the gridiron may come from a misreading or a literary flourish; they think he was more likely beheaded. This is labelled as tradition.[3][11][9]
  • Became one of the most honoured martyrs of Rome: the Liber Pontificalis credits Constantine with building a basilica over his tomb (the origin of San Lorenzo fuori le Mura), and he is one of the saints named in the Roman Canon of the Mass.[5][11]
  • Prudentius's hymn to him (Peristephanon 2) presents his death as the turning point that led Rome, including senators, to abandon the old gods; this is a poetic claim, not a historical record.[4][9]
  • Was the subject of sermons by Augustine (Sermons 302–305) and by Leo the Great, who said Rome was made as illustrious by Lawrence as Jerusalem was by Stephen.[6][7]
  • Is patron of deacons, of the poor, of cooks (because of the gridiron story), and of Rome alongside Peter and Paul. The Perseid meteors around his feast are called the 'tears of St Lawrence', and Philip II of Spain dedicated the Escorial to him after the victory at Saint-Quentin on his feast day, 10 August 1557.[11][10]

Words and sayings

Lawrence left no writings. Every word attributed to him comes from Ambrose and Prudentius, writing more than a century after his death, so all are listed as traditional rather than his own recorded words.

Persecution they supported or carried out

No record of Lawrence supporting or carrying out persecution of anyone. In the traditional accounts his part in the persecution was as its target, refusing to hand over church property.

Persecution, arrests, and executions

Lawrence was killed in the persecution of 257–258 under the emperors Valerian and Gallienus, four days after his bishop. The persecution and his death date are historical; the details of his interrogation and execution come from later tradition. The early accounts do not name the official who condemned him, and later legends (such as the Golden Legend) wrongly set the story under the emperor Decius.

Excommunications

Never excommunicated or condemned by any church body. He has been venerated as a saint and martyr in the Catholic, Orthodox, and Anglican traditions since antiquity.

6 August 258

Execution of his bishop, Sixtus II, and fellow deacons

Under Valerian's second rescript, which ordered bishops, presbyters, and deacons to be executed at once, Sixtus II was seized while meeting in a cemetery and put to death, as Cyprian of Carthage reported within weeks. Several of the Roman deacons died with him or soon after. Ambrose's later account has Lawrence following Sixtus to his execution and being told he would follow in three days.[1][3][5]

6–10 August 258

Arrest, interrogation over the church's wealth, and execution

By tradition, the authorities, believing the church held great wealth, ordered Lawrence as its steward to hand it over; he asked for time, gave the money to the poor, and brought them before the official as the church's treasures, and was then condemned and roasted on a gridiron. Ambrose and Prudentius tell the story more than a century later; neither names the official, whom Prudentius calls the prefect of the city. Many historians think he was beheaded, the penalty Valerian's rescript prescribed. The date of his death, 10 August 258, is historical.[3][4][2][11]
